The Maths You Actually Need for Class 12 Physics

Ten short blocks, in the order you should teach them. Every rule is followed by one worked example and 4–6 practice questions, so you can check understanding before moving on. Nothing here assumes prior comfort with maths.

How to run this session: Go block by block. Don't move to the next block until the student can do the practice questions for the current one unaided — everything after Block 07 (graphs) depends on Blocks 01–07 being solid. Answers are hidden under "Show answer" — get the student to attempt on paper first.
Block 01

§ Algebra Basics — Combining & Simplifying Terms

In physics, letters like u, v, a, t, m stand for real quantities (speed, time, mass...). "Doing algebra" just means combining these quantities correctly — same idea as combining apples with apples.

Like terms are terms with the exact same letter part. Only like terms can be added or subtracted directly.

Rule
3x + 5x = 8x   (like terms → add the numbers in front, "coefficients")
3x + 5y → cannot combine. Stays 3x + 5y
7m² + 2m² = 9m²   (m² and m² are like terms)
7m² + 2m → cannot combine (different powers of m)

Transposition — moving a term across the "=" sign flips its sign (+ becomes −, and vice versa). This is how we rearrange a physics formula to solve for a different quantity.

Worked Example
Solve for v in: v − u = at
Move −u to the other side → sign flips to +u
v = u + at — this is the first equation of motion!
Rearranging v = u + at to find t = (v − u) / a is transposition — you'll do this constantly in Kinematics, Newton's Laws, and Current Electricity.
Practice — Block 01
  1. Simplify: 4x + 9x − 2x
    Show answer
    11x
  2. Simplify: 5a² + 3a − 2a² + 7a
    Show answer
    3a² + 10a
  3. Rearrange s = ut + ½at² to isolate u (treat ½at² as one block).
    Show answer
    u = (s − ½at²) / t
  4. Rearrange F = ma to find a.
    Show answer
    a = F / m
  5. Solve for x: 3x + 7 = 22
    Show answer
    3x = 15 → x = 5
  6. Rearrange V = IR to find R.
    Show answer
    R = V / I
Block 02

§ Laws of Powers & Exponents

A power just means "multiply this many times": a³ = a × a × a. Physics constantly uses powers — for units (m², m³), for formulas (v² = u² + 2as), and for very large/small numbers (10⁸, 10⁻¹⁹).

The Four Rules You Must Know Cold
1. Multiplying same base → ADD the powers: aᵐ × aⁿ = aᵐ⁺ⁿ
2. Dividing same base → SUBTRACT the powers: aᵐ ÷ aⁿ = aᵐ⁻ⁿ
3. Power of a power → MULTIPLY the powers: (aᵐ)ⁿ = aᵐⁿ
4. Negative power → flip to denominator: a⁻ⁿ = 1 / aⁿ
Also: a⁰ = 1 (anything to the power 0 is 1)    a^(1/2) = √a
Worked Example
Simplify: (2 × 10³) × (3 × 10⁵)
Multiply the plain numbers: 2 × 3 = 6
Add the powers of 10: 10³ × 10⁵ = 10⁸
Answer: 6 × 10⁸
Scientific notation (like 3 × 10⁸ m/s for the speed of light, or 1.6 × 10⁻¹⁹ C for electron charge) is used on almost every page of your Physics book. If powers aren't automatic, every numerical problem becomes slow.
Practice — Block 02
  1. Simplify: 10⁴ × 10³
    Show answer
    10⁷
  2. Simplify: 10⁶ ÷ 10²
    Show answer
    10⁴
  3. Simplify: (10³)²
    Show answer
    10⁶
  4. Write 0.000045 in scientific notation.
    Show answer
    4.5 × 10⁻⁵
  5. Simplify: (4 × 10⁵) × (2 × 10⁻²)
    Show answer
    8 × 10³
  6. Simplify: m³ × m⁻¹ ÷ m²
    Show answer
    m^(3−1−2) = m⁰ = 1
Block 03

§ Quadratic Equations

A quadratic equation has a squared term: ax² + bx + c = 0. In physics, these show up whenever you solve kinematics equations for time, or projectile problems for range/height.

The Quadratic Formula
For ax² + bx + c = 0:
x = [ −b ± √(b² − 4ac) ] / 2a
This always gives 2 answers — in physics, reject the one that's negative time or physically impossible.
Worked Example
A ball's height: s = 20t − 5t². Find t when s = 15.
15 = 20t − 5t² → 5t² − 20t + 15 = 0 → divide by 5 → t² − 4t + 3 = 0
Using formula: a=1, b=−4, c=3 → t = [4 ± √(16−12)] / 2 = [4 ± 2] / 2
t = 3 or t = 1 → both valid (ball passes height 15 m twice — going up and coming down)
Equations like s = ut + ½at² become quadratic in t whenever you're asked "at what time does the object reach height s?" — very common in projectile motion questions.
Practice — Block 03
  1. Solve: x² − 5x + 6 = 0
    Show answer
    (x−2)(x−3)=0 → x = 2 or x = 3
  2. Solve: t² − 7t + 10 = 0
    Show answer
    t = 2 or t = 5
  3. Solve using the formula: 2x² + 3x − 2 = 0
    Show answer
    x = [−3 ± √(9+16)]/4 = [−3±5]/4 → x = 0.5 or x = −2
  4. A stone is thrown: s = 40t − 5t². Find t when s = 60 m.
    Show answer
    5t²−40t+60=0 → t²−8t+12=0 → t=2 or t=6
Block 04

§ Trigonometry Basics

Trigonometry connects angles to ratios of sides in a right-angled triangle. Physics uses it to break a slanted force, velocity, or displacement into horizontal and vertical parts.

SOH-CAH-TOA (for a right triangle, angle θ)
sin θ = Opposite / Hypotenuse
cos θ = Adjacent / Hypotenuse
tan θ = Opposite / Adjacent = sin θ / cos θ
θ0°30°45°60°90°
sin θ01/21/√2√3/21
cos θ1√3/21/√21/20
tan θ01/√31√3∞

Small-angle approximation: for very small θ (in radians), sin θ ≈ θ and tan θ ≈ θ. Used in simple pendulum / SHM chapters.

Worked Example
A force of 10 N acts at 30° above the horizontal. Find its horizontal and vertical parts.
Horizontal: F cos30° = 10 × (√3/2) = 8.66 N
Vertical: F sin30° = 10 × (1/2) = 5 N
Resolving vectors, projectile motion (range = u²sin2θ/g), inclined planes, and AC circuits (phasors) all lean on this table. Memorise it — don't re-derive it every time.
Practice — Block 04
  1. A ladder makes 60° with the ground and is 4 m long. Find its horizontal distance from the wall (cos60° = 1/2).
    Show answer
    4 × cos60° = 4 × 0.5 = 2 m
  2. Resolve a velocity of 20 m/s at 45° into horizontal and vertical components.
    Show answer
    Both = 20 × (1/√2) ≈ 14.14 m/s
  3. If sin θ = 1/2, what is θ?
    Show answer
    30°
  4. A block on an incline of 30° — write the component of gravity (mg) along the incline.
    Show answer
    mg sin30° = mg/2
Block 05

§ Vectors & Components

A vector has both size (magnitude) and direction — like velocity or force. A scalar has only size — like mass or time. Vectors can't just be added like plain numbers unless they point the same way.

Core Rules
Magnitude of vector (x, y): |V| = √(x² + y²) — this is just Pythagoras' theorem
Adding vectors at right angles: R = √(A² + B²)
Components: Vx = V cosθ,   Vy = V sinθ (θ measured from x-axis)
Worked Example
Find the resultant of a 3 N force (east) and a 4 N force (north).
These are perpendicular → R = √(3² + 4²) = √25 = 5 N
Every "resolve the forces" or "find the resultant velocity" question — Laws of Motion, Projectile Motion, Work-Energy — is really Pythagoras + the sinθ/cosθ table from Block 04.
Practice — Block 05
  1. Find the magnitude of a vector with components (6, 8).
    Show answer
    √(36+64) = √100 = 10
  2. Two forces, 5 N and 12 N, act at right angles. Find the resultant.
    Show answer
    √(25+144) = √169 = 13 N
  3. A velocity of 10 m/s acts at 60° to the horizontal. Find the vertical component. (sin60° = √3/2)
    Show answer
    10 × √3/2 ≈ 8.66 m/s
Block 06

§ Summation — The Σ (Sigma) Symbol

Σ is just shorthand for "add up all of these." Instead of writing a long list of terms, we write one line telling the reader what to add and over what range.

Reading Σ
Σ (i = 1 to n) i  means  1 + 2 + 3 + ... + n
Σ (i = 1 to 4) i²  means  1² + 2² + 3² + 4² = 1+4+9+16 = 30
Σ mᵢxᵢ  means  m₁x₁ + m₂x₂ + m₃x₃ + ... (add each mass times its position)
Worked Example
Centre of mass formula: Xcm = Σ(mᵢxᵢ) / Σmᵢ
For 2 masses: m₁=2kg at x₁=0m, m₂=3kg at x₂=5m
Σmᵢxᵢ = (2×0) + (3×5) = 15    Σmᵢ = 2+3 = 5
Xcm = 15/5 = 3 m
Σ appears directly in the Centre of Mass / System of Particles chapter, and conceptually behind every "total force," "total charge," or "total work done by many forces" statement.
Practice — Block 06
  1. Write out and evaluate: Σ (i = 1 to 5) i
    Show answer
    1+2+3+4+5 = 15
  2. Evaluate: Σ (i = 1 to 3) i²
    Show answer
    1+4+9 = 14
  3. Three masses: 1 kg at x=0, 2 kg at x=2, 3 kg at x=4. Find Xcm.
    Show answer
    Σmx = 0+4+12=16, Σm=6 → Xcm = 16/6 ≈ 2.67 m
Block 07

§ Graphs — Slope & Area (the bridge to calculus)

Before differentiation and integration make sense as symbols, they need to make sense as pictures. This block is the bridge.

Two Big Ideas
1. SLOPE of a graph = "how steep" = rise / run = Δy / Δx
2. AREA under a graph = the product of the two axes, added up over the range
Worked Example — position-time graph
A car's position goes from 10 m to 50 m in 4 s. Slope = (50−10)/4 = 10 m/s
Slope of position-time graph = velocity. This is exactly what differentiation calculates.
Worked Example — velocity-time graph
A car moves at constant 20 m/s for 5 s. Area under graph = 20 × 5 = 100 m
Area under velocity-time graph = distance travelled. This is exactly what integration calculates.
Once a student accepts "slope = rate of change" and "area = total accumulated," differentiation and integration stop being mysterious symbols — they're just slope and area for curves instead of straight lines.
Practice — Block 07
  1. A position-time graph goes from 0 m to 60 m in 3 s (straight line). Find the velocity.
    Show answer
    60/3 = 20 m/s
  2. A velocity-time graph is a horizontal line at 15 m/s for 10 s. Find distance travelled.
    Show answer
    Area = 15 × 10 = 150 m
  3. A velocity-time graph rises in a straight line from 0 to 20 m/s in 4 s. Find the distance travelled (hint: area of triangle = ½ × base × height).
    Show answer
    ½ × 4 × 20 = 40 m
Block 08

§ Differentiation — Finding the Rate of Change

Differentiation finds the slope of a curve at every point — i.e. how fast one quantity changes with another. In physics, we almost always differentiate with respect to time.

The Power Rule (the only rule you need for most of Class 12)
d/dx (xⁿ) = n·xⁿ⁻¹   → bring the power down, subtract 1 from the power
d/dx (constant) = 0   (a constant never changes, so its rate of change is 0)
d/dx (k·xⁿ) = k·n·xⁿ⁻¹   (constants just tag along, multiplied through)
Sum rule: d/dx (f + g) = d/dx(f) + d/dx(g)   (differentiate term by term)
Two extra results used in Class 12
d/dx (sin x) = cos x
d/dx (cos x) = −sin x
Worked Example
Position: x = 5t² + 2t + 3. Find velocity v = dx/dt.
d/dt(5t²) = 5×2×t¹ = 10t
d/dt(2t) = 2    d/dt(3) = 0
v = 10t + 2
v = dx/dt (velocity is rate of change of position) and a = dv/dt (acceleration is rate of change of velocity) are used everywhere: Kinematics, SHM, Electromagnetic Induction (EMF = −dΦ/dt), and AC current.
Practice — Block 08
  1. Differentiate: y = x³
    Show answer
    dy/dx = 3x²
  2. Differentiate: y = 4x² + 3x + 7
    Show answer
    dy/dx = 8x + 3
  3. x = 3t² − 4t + 1. Find velocity v = dx/dt.
    Show answer
    v = 6t − 4
  4. v = 6t − 4 (from above). Find acceleration a = dv/dt.
    Show answer
    a = 6 (constant)
  5. Differentiate: y = 5 sin x
    Show answer
    dy/dx = 5 cos x
Block 09

§ Integration — Adding Up Infinitely Small Pieces

Integration is the reverse of differentiation. Where differentiation finds slope, integration finds the area under a curve — i.e. it "un-does" a rate to recover the total.

The Power Rule for Integration
∫ xⁿ dx = xⁿ⁺¹ / (n+1) + C   → add 1 to the power, then divide by the new power
∫ k dx = kx + C   (a constant, integrated, becomes k times x)
"+C" is added because when you differentiate any constant, you get 0 — so integration can't tell what constant was originally there. In physics, C is fixed using initial conditions.
Definite Integral (used for area between two limits)
∫ (a to b) f(x) dx = [F(x)] from a to b = F(b) − F(a)
Worked Example
Velocity: v = 4t + 2. Find displacement x = ∫v dt from t=0 to t=3.
∫(4t + 2) dt = 4t²/2 + 2t = 2t² + 2t
At t=3: 2(9) + 2(3) = 18 + 6 = 24 m    At t=0: 0
Displacement = 24 − 0 = 24 m
x = ∫v dt and v = ∫a dt recover displacement/velocity from rates — used in Kinematics with variable acceleration, Work done by a variable force (W = ∫F dx), and charge from variable current (q = ∫I dt).
Practice — Block 09
  1. Integrate: ∫ x² dx
    Show answer
    x³/3 + C
  2. Integrate: ∫ (3x + 5) dx
    Show answer
    3x²/2 + 5x + C
  3. v = 2t + 3. Find displacement from t=0 to t=2 using x = ∫v dt.
    Show answer
    ∫(2t+3)dt = t²+3t → at t=2: 4+6=10; at t=0: 0 → displacement = 10 m
  4. Force F = 2x (varies with position). Find work done W = ∫F dx from x=0 to x=3.
    Show answer
    ∫2x dx = x² → at x=3: 9; at x=0: 0 → W = 9 J
