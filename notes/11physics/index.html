Motion in a Straight Line & Motion in a Plane

1. Necessary Formulas

Motion in a Straight Line

  • Average Velocity: \( v_{avg} = \frac{\Delta x}{\Delta t} \)
  • Instantaneous Velocity: \( v = \frac{dx}{dt} \)
  • Equations of Motion (Constant \(a\)):
    \( v = u + at \)
    \( s = ut + \frac{1}{2}at^2 \)
    \( v^2 = u^2 + 2as \)
  • Stopping Distance: \( d_s = \frac{-u^2}{2a} \)

Motion in a Plane & Vectors

  • Vector Magnitude: \( |\vec{A}| = \sqrt{A_x^2 + A_y^2} \)
  • Projectile Motion:
    Time of Flight: \( T = \frac{2u \sin\theta}{g} \)
    Max Height: \( H = \frac{u^2 \sin^2\theta}{2g} \)
    Horizontal Range: \( R = \frac{u^2 \sin 2\theta}{g} \)
  • Centripetal Acceleration: \( a_c = \frac{v^2}{r} = \omega^2 r \)

2. Topic-wise Practice Questions

Topic A: Motion in a Straight Line

Q1. A car travels half the distance with constant velocity \(40 \, \text{km/h}\) and the remaining half with constant velocity \(60 \, \text{km/h}\). Find the average velocity of the car.

Solution: Let total distance be \(2x\).
Time for first half \(t_1 = \frac{x}{40}\). Time for second half \(t_2 = \frac{x}{60}\).
\(V_{avg} = \frac{\text{Total Distance}}{\text{Total Time}} = \frac{2x}{\frac{x}{40} + \frac{x}{60}}\)
\(V_{avg} = \frac{2 \times 40 \times 60}{40 + 60} = \frac{4800}{100} = \mathbf{48 \, \text{km/h}}\).

Q2. The position of an object is given by \(x = 3t^2 + 2t + 5\). Find its instantaneous velocity at \(t = 2 \, \text{s}\).

Solution: Velocity \(v = \frac{dx}{dt}\).
\(v = \frac{d}{dt}(3t^2 + 2t + 5) = 6t + 2\).
At \(t = 2\): \(v = 6(2) + 2 = \mathbf{14 \, \text{unit/s}}\).

Topic B: Motion in a Plane

Q3. A projectile is fired at an angle of \(30^\circ\) with the horizontal with a velocity of \(98 \, \text{m/s}\). Calculate its time of flight. (Take \(g = 9.8 \, \text{m/s}^2\))

Solution: \(T = \frac{2u \sin\theta}{g}\)
\(T = \frac{2 \times 98 \times \sin 30^\circ}{9.8} = \frac{2 \times 98 \times 0.5}{9.8}\)
\(T = \frac{98}{9.8} = \mathbf{10 \, \text{seconds}}\).

Q4. A particle moves in a circle of radius \(5 \, \text{m}\) with a constant speed of \(10 \, \text{m/s}\). What is the magnitude of its centripetal acceleration?

Solution: \(a_c = \frac{v^2}{r}\)
\(a_c = \frac{10^2}{5} = \frac{100}{5} = \mathbf{20 \, \text{m/s}^2}\).
